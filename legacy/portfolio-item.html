Project write-up

This portfolio, as a model card

Why I rebuilt my own site around an AI/ML theme, and how the live background and two mini-games actually work.

role designer & developer stack html · css · js · canvas status shipped

The idea

I'm a CS student who spends most of my free time reading about machine learning, so I wanted my portfolio to say that without just listing it as a bullet point. Instead of a typical "about / skills / projects" template, the whole site is framed as a model card — the kind of short documentation you'd write before shipping a model — and the projects section is called "inference examples" instead of a plain gallery.

The signature: a live perceptron trainer

The centerpiece is a small interactive playground where you can click to place points into two classes and then watch a linear perceptron learn a decision boundary in real time, epoch by epoch. It's a genuinely working (if intentionally simple) implementation: each training step nudges the weights for every misclassified point, redraws the boundary line, and reports live accuracy until the classes are separated or the epoch limit is hit.

Ambient neural network background

Behind the hero text, a canvas animation scatters nodes that drift slowly and connect to nearby neighbors, with connections brightening near the cursor to suggest activation. It's decorative, but it's built from the same idea as the trainer below it — small units, connected, responding to input.

What I'd add next

  • A second, non-linear playground (a tiny 2-layer network) to show what the perceptron can't do.
  • Persisting high scores for the memory-match game across visits.
  • Write-ups for the quiz app and the database-backed project, in the same model-card voice.
